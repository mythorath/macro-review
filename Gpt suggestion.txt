est technical companion: an IQA / aesthetic model

What you want here is an Image Quality Assessment model.

These are not usually run through Ollama.
They are usually run through Python + PyTorch.

Good model families to look at

These are the ones I’d look at first:

NIMA
classic aesthetic scoring model
good for “how pleasing is this image?”
older, but simple and useful
MUSIQ
good no-reference image quality assessment
useful for technical quality perception
MANIQA
strong modern no-reference IQA model
good for perceptual quality
TOPIQ
good for technical/perceptual image quality
HyperIQA
another strong no-reference IQA option
CLIP-IQA / Q-Align style approaches
useful if you want quality aligned more with human preference or language guidance
What these models are good at

They can help score:

sharpness
blur
noise
exposure issues
overall perceived quality
aesthetic appeal
What they are not good at

They usually won’t tell you:

“the left side is too heavy visually”
“the insect’s eye should be the critical focus point”
“this stem merging into the wing is distracting”

That’s where Qwen or another VLM comes in.

What I’d recommend specifically
Best overall local setup on your 5090
1. Use Qwen as the critique engine

Ask it to analyze:

composition
focus placement
lighting
background separation
subject isolation
framing
color harmony
distracting elements
edit suggestions
2. Use one IQA model as the score engine

I’d start with:

MANIQA or MUSIQ for quality
NIMA if you want aesthetic scoring too
3. Combine them

Let the IQA model give you:

technical score
aesthetic score

Then let Qwen explain:

why the image succeeded or failed

That’s the sweet spot.

For macro insect photography specifically

Macro is weird, because a “good” macro photo is not always what generic photo models think is good.

For insect macro, your analysis should care about:

Technical macro-specific factors
Is the eye critically sharp?
Is the sharpness on the right body part?
Is shallow DOF helping or hurting?
Are important structures lost to blur?
Is motion blur present?
Are there focus stacking artifacts?
Is diffraction softening detail?
Is flash harsh or well controlled?
Are highlights clipped on shiny shell/eyes/wings?
Is the background smooth or distracting?
Composition-specific factors
Subject placement
Viewing angle
Clear separation from background
Direction of subject gaze / body orientation
Negative space
Leading lines from stems or branches
Whether the frame feels cramped
Whether important appendages are cut off
Whether the pose feels dynamic or static

Generic AI often misses the eye sharpness priority, so you’ll want to prompt for that explicitly.
